The share of Claude conversations classified as automation rather than augmentation. We aggregate shares across countries to an EU average. This yields 0.46-0.47 vs. US 0.49-0.50 (ratio 0.94). So the automation shares do not differ widely. But we re-interpret what they mean. Computed by compute_values/compute_psi.py; extract in data/aei_automation_by_country.csv. The automation share is the share of conversations where the user hands the task to Claude and takes its output, rather than getting help with their own work (about half of all conversations). We interpret this less optimistically than Antropic, and consider it closer to an upper bound. The worker still specifies, checks, and iterates on that output. In the model, automation means the worker's time on the task goes to zero. So each scenario moves down one step:

  • modest 0.25: half the conversation share
  • substantial 0.50: the actual conversation share
  • extreme 0.75: the API share

This is also consistent with use being mostly assistive. Humlum and Vestergaard (2025) report just 3% time changes, and Chatterji et al. (2025) note that 'drafting' is among the most common work done by ChatGPT Alternative is to stick to Antropic's 0.50 / 0.75 / 0.90.

Wages in Europe change every +- 15 months on average and relative pay is fixed in collective agreements covering about 60% of workers. In the US wages change faster, every +- 12 months, and only 12% of workers are covered by collective agreements.

Sources: Druant et al. (2009), Barattieri et al. (2014), OECD/AIAS ICTWSS database, https://www.oecd.org/en/data/datasets/ecdaias-ictwss-database.html

This captures the persistence of cognitive wages above their market-clearing level. Wages change less often in Europe, so the value should be higher than in the US.

  • likely a lower bound because collective agreements add additional drag, so could also check 0.7
