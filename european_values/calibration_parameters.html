ε epsCalibration.eps how strongly the capital stock expands when the return to capital rises 3

half the long-run wealth elasticity of Moll et al. (2022)

 retain 3

Antropic / half of Moll, Rachel and Restrepo (2022)

This parameter is important for several reasons

  • it is a judgement value and not an estimate
  • it is the parameter the wage-capital split is most sensitive to
  • deciding on a value is not trivial so we should probably keep a range like Antropic

Some further notes:

  • there is no EU estimate of this reduced-form elasticity or of the underlying wealth elasticity
  • not trivial to obtain either: needs household panel with wealth info and a shock to returns
  • the US value itself is not estimated either: Moll et al. pick a preference parameter to hit a long-run semi-elasticity, we would need a full European recalibration and credible semi-elasticity target
  • difficult to even guess whether it would be lower/higher as there are offsetting arguments:
  • we are a net capital exporter (more elastic)
  • but have more shallow capital markets (less elastic).

GDP without AI grows at g + n. The Commission estimates the EU economy can grow 1.3-1.4% a year We already have g = 1.27% (TFP growth / labour share). Leaves about 0.1% a year for the labour force, so n = 0.001. Check against labour-force data: the EU labour force grew 0.6% a year over 2015-2024 and the Commission forecasts +0.45% a year to 2027 (AMECO NLTN); but the population aged 20-64 is projected to shrink 0.37% a year to 2030 (Eurostat EUROPOP2023) and the 2024 Ageing Report has the labour force falling 0.3% a year in the long run. Sources: https://db.nomics.world/AMECO/OVGDP, https://db.nomics.world/AMECO/NLTN, https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/databrowser/view/proj_23np

Antropic does the same for the US: pick n so GDP without AI grows 2% a year. They note that BLS projects labour-force growth of about 0.4%. Here n is picked so that GDP without AI grows at the Commission's 1.3-1.4%.

  • the data checks point in the opposite directions: the labour force has been growing (more
